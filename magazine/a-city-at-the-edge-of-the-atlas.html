A City at the Edge of the Atlas

Ouarzazate, between kasbahs, film sets and one of the largest solar plants in the world.

Places · Story[Author name]30 September 20262 min read

Ouarzazate sits where the High Atlas gives way to the pre-Saharan valleys. For centuries it was a crossing point: caravans heading south towards the Drâa valley and the desert passed through, and earthen kasbahs grew along the routes.

The kasbah of Taourirt, in the centre of town, is the best known. Its towers and courtyards, built from rammed earth and mudbrick, need constant care: rain that would barely mark a stone wall can bring part of an earthen one down.

In the twentieth century, the light and the landscapes brought another kind of visitor. Film crews arrived, studios were built on the edge of town, and Ouarzazate became a stand-in for deserts and ancient cities in productions from around the world. Not far away, the fortified village of Aït Benhaddou was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1987.

The same sun that baked the kasbahs now powers the country.

The newest landmark is outside the city: the Noor solar complex, whose mirrors and panels spread across the plateau. The same sun that baked the kasbahs now produces electricity for the country. Ouarzazate is a good place to see how Morocco carries its past and its present at the same time.

Key dates

  1. 1987Aït Benhaddou is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site.
  2. 2016The first phase of the Noor solar complex starts producing power.
