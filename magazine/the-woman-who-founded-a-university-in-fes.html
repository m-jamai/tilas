The Woman Who Founded a University in Fès

Fatima al-Fihri, al-Qarawiyyin, and what the sources really tell us.

People · Profile[Author name]3 October 20262 min read

In the ninth century, a family of merchants from Kairouan, in today’s Tunisia, settled in Fès, then a young city on the banks of the Oued Fès. According to tradition, when the father died, one of his daughters, Fatima al-Fihri, used her inheritance to build a mosque for the growing quarter of migrants from Kairouan.

That mosque became al-Qarawiyyin. Over the centuries it grew into a centre of teaching in law, theology, grammar, astronomy and medicine, and it is often described today as the oldest university still in operation.

A mosque built for one neighbourhood became a school for centuries.

Much of what we know about Fatima comes from a single chronicle, written by Ibn Abi Zar’ in the fourteenth century, roughly five hundred years after the events. Historians still debate the details. The story is also remembered through her sister, Maryam, to whom tradition attributes the Andalusian mosque on the other bank of the river.

What is not in doubt is the building itself: enlarged under the Almoravids, restored many times, and still at the centre of the medina. Its library, one of the oldest in the world, reopened after restoration in 2016.

Timeline

  1. 859Tradition dates the founding of the mosque to this year.
  2. 12th centuryThe Almoravids enlarge the building.
  3. 1963Al-Qarawiyyin becomes part of Morocco’s modern university system.
  4. 2016The restored library reopens.
