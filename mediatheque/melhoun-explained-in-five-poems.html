About this work

Melhoun is sung poetry in Moroccan Arabic. Its long poems, called qasidas, are built around a refrain that the chorus answers, and they speak of love, faith, cities, craft and everyday life.

The tradition is usually traced to the Tafilalet region, and it flourished in the cities of Fès, Meknes, Marrakech and Salé, often among craftsmen. Many of its best-known poets were artisans themselves.

In this episode, five poems serve as five doors into the form: how a qasida is built, who wrote them, who sings them today, and why the words matter as much as the music. In 2023, UNESCO added melhoun to its list of the intangible cultural heritage of humanity.

In this episode

  1. What melhoun is
  2. How a qasida is built
  3. The poets, many of them craftsmen
  4. Who sings it today
  5. Five poems, one by one
