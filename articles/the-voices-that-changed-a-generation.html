The Voices That Changed a Generation

Casablanca in the 1970s, and the groups that sang in the language of the street.

Culture[Author name]6 October 202622 min read

In the early 1970s, something shifted in the music coming out of Casablanca’s working-class neighbourhoods. Young groups set aside the orchestras and polished love songs heard on the radio, and picked up instruments from closer to home: the banjo, the bendir, the guembri.

They sang in darija, the Arabic people actually spoke, and borrowed from gnawa ceremonies, Sufi chant and the poetry of melhoun. The songs were short on romance and long on questions: about work, about loss, about who gets to speak.

The songs were short on romance and long on questions.

This story follows that moment from the rooms where it started to the cassettes that carried it across the country.
